**Self-Attention** is the core mechanism of the Transformer architecture. It allows a word to look at every other word in a sentence to figure out the context.

However, a single word can have multiple different relationships in a sentence. 
Consider the sentence: *"The fierce dog chased the frightened cat."*
The word *"chased"* has two distinct relationships:
1.  **Who** did the chasing? (The dog).
2.  **Who** was chased? (The cat).

If the neural network only has one "Attention Mechanism," it gets confused. It tries to mathematically link *"chased"* to both *"dog"* and *"cat"* simultaneously, mushing the math together into an average that doesn't clearly represent either concept.

## The Solution: Multi-Head Attention

The authors of the 2017 Transformer paper solved this by using **Multi-Head Attention**.

Instead of performing the Self-Attention calculation once, the neural network splits the data and performs the exact same calculation multiple times in parallel. Each parallel calculation is called an **"Attention Head."**

If a model has 8 Attention Heads, the word *"chased"* gets 8 distinct opportunities to look around the sentence. 

### Specialization
Because the neural network is trained on massive amounts of data, the different "Heads" naturally learn to specialize in different types of linguistic relationships, completely on their own without human intervention.

*   **Head 1** might mathematically dedicate itself to finding the Subject of the sentence (linking *"chased"* to *"dog"*).
*   **Head 2** might dedicate itself to finding the Object of the sentence (linking *"chased"* to *"cat"*).
*   **Head 3** might focus entirely on adjectives, linking *"dog"* to *"fierce"*.
*   **Head 8** might focus entirely on punctuation or physical distance between words.

By having dozens of specialized heads looking at the sentence from different perspectives simultaneously, the model gains an incredibly deep, multi-faceted understanding of the text.

## The Mathematics of the Split

If the neural network is operating in a 512-dimensional space, and it has 8 Attention Heads:
1.  The model does *not* do the math in 512 dimensions 8 separate times (that would be incredibly slow).
2.  Instead, it mathematically slices the 512 dimensions into 8 smaller chunks of 64 dimensions each ($512 / 8 = 64$).
3.  Each of the 8 heads performs the attention calculation in its own tiny 64-dimensional space.
4.  Once all 8 heads are finished, the results are concatenated (glued) back together into a single 512-dimensional vector.

This brilliant mathematical trick means that Multi-Head Attention takes the exact same amount of computational time as Single-Head attention, but provides vastly superior reasoning capabilities.

<Callout icon="layer-group" title="Modern Scale">
The original 2017 Transformer used 8 Attention Heads. Modern Large Language Models take this to the extreme. OpenAI's GPT-3 (175B parameters) uses **96 separate Attention Heads** per layer, allowing it to track 96 distinct semantic and syntactic relationships for every single word it processes.
</Callout>
