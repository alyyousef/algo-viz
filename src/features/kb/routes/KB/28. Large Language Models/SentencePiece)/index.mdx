Neural networks do not understand human words, letters, or strings. They only understand raw mathematics. 

Before you can feed the sentence *"I love AI"* into a Large Language Model, you must pass it through a **Tokenizer**. A Tokenizer is a rigid software dictionary that chops the text into tiny chunks (Tokens) and assigns a unique ID number to each chunk.

## Why not just use words?

Why not just make every word in the English dictionary a token? 
Because language is infinitely complex. If a model encounters a word it has never seen before (like *"supercalifragilistic"* or a complex URL), it would crash. Furthermore, standard dictionaries don't account for prefixes (un-), suffixes (-ing), or punctuation.

## Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE)

The most popular tokenization algorithm in the world (used by OpenAI for GPT-3/GPT-4) is **BPE (Byte-Pair Encoding)**.

BPE is a data compression algorithm. Instead of hard-coding words, BPE starts by breaking a massive dataset of text down into individual letters. It then scans the data to find the most frequent pairs of characters and mathematically merges them into a new token.

1.  It notices that "e" and "r" appear next to each other millions of times. It merges them into a new token: `"er"`.
2.  It notices that "t" and "h" appear constantly. It merges them: `"th"`.
3.  Eventually, it builds up tokens that represent full words (`"apple"`), but it also retains the sub-word chunks (`"pre"`, `"ing"`, `"tion"`).

This means if BPE encounters a completely alien word like *"pretraining"*, it doesn't crash. It simply breaks it down into familiar sub-word tokens: `["pre", "train", "ing"]`.

## SentencePiece

While BPE is brilliant, it has a fatal flaw: it assumes that words are separated by spaces.
This completely breaks down when processing languages like Chinese or Japanese, which do not use spaces between words, or when processing complex Python code with highly specific whitespace formatting.

**SentencePiece** (developed by Google and used in LLaMA and Mistral) solves this. 
SentencePiece treats the entire input string (including spaces, tabs, and newlines) as a raw, continuous stream of data. It explicitly treats the "space" character as just another mathematical token (often represented as `_`).

This allows SentencePiece to perfectly tokenize any language, code, or formatting structure without relying on human grammatical rules.

<Callout icon="coins" title="The Economics of Tokenization">
When you pay for an LLM API (like OpenAI), you do not pay per word; you pay per token. On average, **1 token is roughly 0.75 English words**. However, if you prompt the model in a rare language (like Icelandic), the Tokenizer struggles and often breaks a single word into 5 or 6 separate tokens. This means running AI in non-English languages is often drastically more expensive.
</Callout>
