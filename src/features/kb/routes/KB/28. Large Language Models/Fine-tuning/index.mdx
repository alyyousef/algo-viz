**Fine-Tuning** is the process of taking a massive, pre-trained Large Language Model (like an open-source LLaMA or Mistral model) and permanently altering its neural network weights by training it on a small, highly specialized dataset.

## Why Fine-Tune? (RAG vs Fine-Tuning)

A common misconception is that Fine-Tuning is used to teach a model *new facts* (e.g., uploading your company's HR handbook). This is incorrect. If you want a model to know new facts, you use **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)**.

Fine-Tuning is used to teach a model a **new behavior, a specific tone, or a rigid structural format.**
*   *Bad Use Case:* Fine-tuning to teach an LLM what happened in the news yesterday.
*   *Good Use Case:* Fine-tuning an LLM to act exactly like a 19th-century pirate, or fine-tuning it to perfectly convert English questions into complex SQL database queries.

## The Stages of Training

To understand Fine-Tuning, you must understand the LLM lifecycle:

1.  **Pre-Training (The Foundation):** The model reads the entire internet for months on a $100 Million supercomputer. It learns grammar, logic, and general world knowledge. It becomes a "Base Model." Base models are useless for chatting; if you prompt a base model with *"What is the capital of France?"*, it might just autocomplete with *"What is the capital of Germany?"*
2.  **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The model is trained on thousands of highly curated, high-quality Q&A pairs written by humans. This teaches the model the *behavior* of being a helpful chatbot. It learns that when a human asks a question, it should provide an answer.
3.  **Alignment (DPO/RLHF):** The final polish, teaching the model not to curse, not to be racist, and not to help build bombs.

When enterprise developers "Fine-Tune" an open-source model, they are generally performing their own specialized version of **Step 2 (SFT)**.

## How SFT Works

You create a dataset of roughly 1,000 to 10,000 examples of exactly how you want the model to behave.
You pass this data through the LLM. The LLM generates a prediction, compares it to your perfect example, calculates the error (Loss), and uses **Backpropagation** to slightly adjust the billions of weights inside its network. 

Once finished, the model is permanently changed. It will now naturally default to your specific format or tone without needing complex prompting.

<Callout icon="microchip" title="Full Fine-Tuning vs PEFT">
Historically, developers used **Full Fine-Tuning**, which updated every single parameter in a 70B model (requiring massive clusters of A100 GPUs). Today, almost all fine-tuning is done using **PEFT (Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning)**, specifically **LoRA**. LoRA freezes the massive base model and only trains a tiny, mathematically injected sub-network, allowing developers to fine-tune massive LLMs on a single consumer gaming GPU.
</Callout>
