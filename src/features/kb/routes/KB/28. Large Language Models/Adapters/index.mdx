**Adapters** were one of the first major breakthroughs in **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)** for Large Language Models. 

When you want an open-source LLM (like LLaMA-3) to learn a highly specific task—like reading medical records and outputting ICD-10 billing codes—you need to fine-tune it.

## The Flaw of Standard Fine-Tuning

In standard "Full Fine-Tuning," you pass your medical data through the network and use backpropagation to slightly adjust every single weight in the network.
If the model has 70 Billion parameters, you are mathematically updating 70 Billion weights. This requires massive clusters of GPUs and generates a brand new 140GB file. If you have 5 different clients who want 5 different tasks, you now have to store five massive 140GB models.

## How Adapters Work

The core idea of an Adapter is simple: **Freeze the original model, and insert tiny new neural networks inside of it.**

1.  **Freeze:** You completely lock all 70 Billion original weights of the pre-trained LLM. They will never change.
2.  **Insert:** You inject tiny, brand-new neural network layers (the "Adapters") directly in between the existing layers of the Transformer block.
3.  **Train:** You pass your medical data through the network. Because the original weights are frozen, backpropagation *only* updates the weights inside the tiny new Adapters.

### The Bottleneck Architecture
To make the Adapters as small as possible, they use a "bottleneck" architecture. 
If the main Transformer layer is 4,096 dimensions wide, the Adapter will forcefully compress the data down to a tiny dimension (e.g., 64 dimensions), process it, and then expand it back to 4,096. This means the Adapter only contains a few million parameters, rather than billions.

## The Benefits

*   **Speed & Hardware:** Because you are only training a few million parameters instead of billions, you can fine-tune massive models on a single consumer GPU in a few hours.
*   **Storage:** The trained Adapter is just a tiny file (often less than 50MB). You keep one copy of the giant base LLM on your server, and you can instantly hot-swap the tiny 50MB Adapter files depending on which client is using the system.

<Callout icon="arrow-right" title="Adapters vs LoRA">
While standard Adapters (inserting new layers between existing ones) were revolutionary when introduced in 2019, they introduced a slight "inference latency" (the data had to travel through extra physical layers, slowing the model down slightly). Today, the industry has almost entirely shifted to **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**, which mathematically injects the new weights *in parallel* to the old weights, achieving the same exact benefits but with zero slowdown during inference.
</Callout>
