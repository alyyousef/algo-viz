In the era of Generative AI, developers rarely write code to solve text-based problems. Instead, they write "Prompts" to instruct a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM) to solve the problem for them.

The two most fundamental methods for prompting an LLM are **Zero-Shot** and **Few-Shot**.

## Zero-Shot Prompting

**Zero-Shot** prompting is exactly what it sounds like: you ask the model to perform a task without giving it a single example of how to do it. You are relying entirely on the generalized knowledge the model learned during its pre-training phase on the internet.

*   **Prompt:** `"Classify the sentiment of this review as Positive, Negative, or Neutral: 'The food was terrible.'"`
*   **Response:** `"Negative."`

This works beautifully on modern, massive models (like GPT-4) for general tasks. However, if the task requires a highly specific output format (e.g., outputting JSON with specific keys) or requires specialized domain logic, Zero-Shot often fails because the model has no reference point for exactly what you want.

## Few-Shot Prompting

**Few-Shot** (also known as In-Context Learning) solves the unreliability of Zero-Shot. Instead of just asking the question, you provide the LLM with 2 to 5 highly curated examples of the *exact* input-output pairs you expect, *before* asking your real question.

*   **Prompt:** 
    `"Review: I loved it! -> Sentiment: Positive"`
    `"Review: Waste of money. -> Sentiment: Negative"`
    `"Review: It was okay. -> Sentiment: Neutral"`
    `"Review: The service was extremely slow and rude. -> Sentiment: "`
*   **Response:** `"Negative"`

### Why Few-Shot is Magical
When you provide examples in the prompt, you are not permanently changing the weights of the neural network (like you do in Fine-Tuning). 

Instead, because the LLM is an autoregressive pattern-matching engine, it reads your examples, temporarily identifies the mathematical pattern in its context window, and strictly applies that pattern to your final question. The moment you clear the chat, the model forgets the pattern entirely.

<Callout icon="lightbulb" title="Best Practices for Few-Shot">
1.  **Variety:** Ensure your examples cover all edge cases (e.g., if you only provide examples of 'Positive' and 'Negative', the model will likely fail if the real answer is 'Neutral').
2.  **Formatting:** The AI will aggressively copy your exact formatting. If your examples use single quotes, the AI will use single quotes. If your examples are bulleted, the AI will use bullets.
3.  **Order Matters:** Research shows LLMs suffer from "Recency Bias." They tend to heavily weight the very last example you provided before your real question.
</Callout>
