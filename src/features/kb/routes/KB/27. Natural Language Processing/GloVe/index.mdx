**GloVe** (Global Vectors for Word Representation) is a famous word embedding algorithm developed by researchers at Stanford University in 2014. 

Alongside Google's Word2Vec, GloVe was one of the two foundational algorithms that defined the era of dense Word Embeddings, permanently replacing older, sparser techniques like Bag of Words or TF-IDF.

## Word2Vec vs GloVe

Both algorithms have the exact same goal: take a dictionary of words and assign a dense vector of numbers (e.g., 300 numbers) to each word, such that words with similar meanings are mathematically close to each other in vector space.

However, they accomplish this using entirely different philosophies:
*   **Word2Vec** uses a *predictive*, local neural network. It slides a tiny window across a sentence and tries to guess the next word based on its immediate neighbors. It only looks at local context.
*   **GloVe** uses a *count-based*, global statistical matrix. It explicitly looks at the entire dataset at once to see the grand, global statistics of how often words appear together.

## How GloVe Works

GloVe is built on a massive spreadsheet called a **Co-occurrence Matrix**. 

### 1. Build the Matrix
Imagine taking the entire English Wikipedia. You create a massive grid where every word is a row, and every word is a column. You slide a window across the text. Every time the word "Ice" appears near the word "Solid," you add $1$ to their intersecting cell in the grid. 

By the end, you have a massive matrix filled with global statistics. The cell for "Ice" and "Solid" will have a massive number (e.g., $10,000$). The cell for "Ice" and "Gas" will have a tiny number. 

### 2. The Ratio of Probabilities
The Stanford researchers realized that the raw counts aren't what matters; the *ratio* of the counts is where the true meaning of words is hidden.

*   Probability of "Solid" appearing near "Ice" = High
*   Probability of "Solid" appearing near "Steam" = Low
*   **The Ratio** (Ice/Steam) = Very High

If the ratio is near $1$, the words are irrelevant (e.g., the word "Water" appears equally near both "Ice" and "Steam", so the ratio is 1). The extreme ratios are what define the semantic meaning of the words.

### 3. Matrix Factorization
Finally, GloVe uses an optimization algorithm to compress this massive, unmanageable matrix down into small, dense, 300-dimensional vectors. It adjusts the numbers inside the 300-D vectors until the mathematical dot product of any two vectors perfectly matches the logarithm of their probability ratio from the massive spreadsheet.

## The Result: Linear Substructures

Because GloVe is explicitly built on ratios, the resulting vector space has incredible, almost magical mathematical properties. The geometric distance between words explicitly represents their semantic relationship.

This is what allows for the famous vector arithmetic:
`Vector("King") - Vector("Man") + Vector("Woman") = Vector("Queen")`

<Callout icon="chart-line" title="The Decline of Static Embeddings">
Both GloVe and Word2Vec are **Static Embeddings**. The word "bank" gets exactly one vector, regardless of whether the sentence is about a river or a financial institution. Because of this, GloVe and Word2Vec have been almost entirely replaced by contextual embeddings (like **BERT** and modern **Transformers**), which dynamically calculate a brand new, unique vector for a word every single time it is used, based on the surrounding sentence.
</Callout>
