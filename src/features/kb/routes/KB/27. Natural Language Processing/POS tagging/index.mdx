**Part-of-Speech (POS) Tagging** is the process of reading a sentence and explicitly labeling every single word with its correct grammatical category (Noun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb, Preposition, etc.).

It is one of the lowest-level, most foundational steps in the traditional NLP pipeline. Almost all higher-level tasks (like Lemmatization, Dependency Parsing, or Information Extraction) completely rely on POS tags to function correctly.

## The Tagset

Modern NLP systems don't just use 8 simple tags (Noun, Verb). They use highly specific, expanded tagsets (most commonly, the **Penn Treebank** tagset, which contains 36 different tags).

For example, it differentiates between:
*   `NN`: Noun, singular (e.g., dog)
*   `NNS`: Noun, plural (e.g., dogs)
*   `VBZ`: Verb, 3rd person singular present (e.g., runs)
*   `VBD`: Verb, past tense (e.g., ran)

**Example Output:**
"The (`DT`) quick (`JJ`) brown (`JJ`) fox (`NN`) jumps (`VBZ`) over (`IN`) the (`DT`) lazy (`JJ`) dog (`NN`)."

## The Core Problem: Ambiguity

If every word had only one part of speech, POS tagging would just be a simple dictionary lookup. The challenge is that English is heavily ambiguous.

Consider the word **"book"**:
*   "I want to read a **book**." $\rightarrow$ Noun (`NN`)
*   "I need to **book** a flight." $\rightarrow$ Verb (`VB`)

To tag the word "book" correctly, the algorithm cannot just look at the word itself; it *must* look at the surrounding context. 

## How POS Taggers Work

Historically, POS tagging followed the standard evolution of NLP algorithms:

1.  **Hidden Markov Models (HMMs):** In the 1990s, HMMs were heavily used. They calculated the pure statistical probability of a sequence of tags. (e.g., If the current word is "the" (`DT`), there is a 90% statistical probability that the next word is a Noun or Adjective, and a 0% probability it is a Verb).
2.  **Conditional Random Fields (CRFs):** CRFs allowed researchers to build in complex "features" (e.g., "If the word ends in '-ly', increase the probability that it is an Adverb (`RB`)").
3.  **Deep Learning:** Modern dedicated POS taggers (like those found in the `spaCy` library) use Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) or LSTMs to achieve near-perfect (97%+) accuracy by implicitly learning the contextual grammar of the language.

<Callout icon="arrow-right" title="Implicit POS in LLMs">
Just like Dependency Parsing, if you are using a modern Transformer like GPT-4, you do not need to run an explicit POS Tagger. The Transformer's self-attention mechanism naturally calculates that "book" is acting as a verb in order to generate the correct next word. Explicit POS tagging is only used today when building strict, deterministic NLP pipelines for data extraction.
</Callout>
