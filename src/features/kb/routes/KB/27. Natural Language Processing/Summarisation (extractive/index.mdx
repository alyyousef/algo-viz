**Text Summarization** in Natural Language Processing is divided into two entirely different paradigms: **Extractive** and **Abstractive**. 

**Extractive Summarization** is the older, simpler, and traditionally more reliable method. It works exactly like a student with a yellow highlighter. The algorithm reads a massive document, identifies the 5 most mathematically "important" sentences, and simply copies and pastes those exact 5 sentences into a new paragraph to form the summary. 

It does not invent new words, and it does not paraphrase.

## How it Works

Because extractive summarization just selects existing sentences, it is essentially treated as a **Ranking Problem**. The algorithm must score every sentence in the document and return the top-N highest-scoring sentences.

Historically, there are two ways to do this:

### 1. Statistical / Graph-Based Methods (e.g., TextRank)
The most famous traditional algorithm is **TextRank** (which is just Google's PageRank algorithm applied to words instead of websites).
1.  Split the document into individual sentences.
2.  Compare every sentence to every other sentence using a mathematical similarity metric (like Cosine Similarity based on TF-IDF vectors).
3.  Build a massive web (a graph). If Sentence A and Sentence B share a lot of the same rare words, draw a strong line connecting them.
4.  Run the PageRank algorithm. A sentence is deemed "important" if it is highly connected to other "important" sentences.
5.  Extract the top 3 highest-scoring sentences.

### 2. Deep Learning Methods
Modern extractive systems use deep neural networks (like BERT). 
You feed the entire document into the neural network, and it performs a simple binary classification on every single sentence: `[1 = Include in Summary]`, `[0 = Do not include]`. Because BERT understands deep semantic context, it is vastly better at ignoring redundant sentences than traditional statistical methods.

## Extractive vs Abstractive

**Abstractive Summarization** (which is what ChatGPT does) involves the AI reading the text and writing a brand new summary from scratch, paraphrasing concepts using words that were never in the original document.

**Why would anyone still use Extractive?**
1.  **Factual Accuracy (No Hallucinations):** Because an extractive model only copies and pastes existing sentences, it is mathematically impossible for it to hallucinate a fake fact, a fake name, or a fake number. 
2.  **Speed:** Extractive algorithms (like TextRank) do not require massive GPUs. They can summarize a 1,000-page book on a standard laptop in seconds.

<Callout icon="triangle-exclamation" title="The Cohesion Problem">
The biggest flaw of Extractive Summarization is a lack of cohesion and flow. Because the algorithm just rips 5 sentences from different parts of a document and jams them together, you frequently end up with jarring summaries like: *"The CEO announced the merger on Tuesday. He said it was a mistake."* (Wait, who is 'He'? The context was in a sentence that the algorithm chose not to extract).
</Callout>
