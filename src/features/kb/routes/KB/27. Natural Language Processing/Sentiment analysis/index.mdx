**Sentiment Analysis** (also known as Opinion Mining) is the process of using NLP to automatically determine the emotional tone or subjective opinion hidden within a piece of text.

Typically, it classifies text into three categories: **Positive**, **Negative**, or **Neutral**.

It is arguably the most widely used NLP application in the corporate world. Companies use it to monitor Twitter for brand reputation, automatically route angry customer emails to managers, and analyze thousands of Amazon product reviews in seconds.

## How it is Solved

### 1. The Lexicon (Rule-Based) Approach
The oldest and simplest method. You create a massive dictionary where every word is assigned a score.
*   "Great" = $+2$
*   "Good" = $+1$
*   "Terrible" = $-2$

To analyze a sentence, you simply add up the scores of all the words. 
**The Flaw:** It completely fails to understand context or negation. If a review says *"This movie was not great,"* a lexicon approach sees the word "great" and blindly scores it as positive. 

### 2. Traditional Machine Learning
In the 2000s, engineers used **Bag of Words** or **TF-IDF** to convert reviews into mathematical vectors, and then trained statistical algorithms (like Naive Bayes or Support Vector Machines) to classify them. 
This fixed the negation problem (if the model saw the bigram "not great" enough times, it learned it was negative). However, it still struggled heavily with sarcasm and nuanced, long-form reviews.

### 3. Deep Learning and Transformers
Today, Sentiment Analysis is dominated by deep neural networks, specifically **Transformers** (like BERT). 
Because Transformers use the Self-Attention mechanism, they read the entire sentence simultaneously. They inherently understand that the sentence *"The design is beautiful, but the battery life is absolutely atrocious"* contains mixed sentiments, and can accurately classify the overall tone based on deep, contextual understanding rather than just keyword counting.

## Advanced Sentiment Analysis

Basic Positive/Negative classification is often not enough for modern analytics.

*   **Fine-grained Sentiment:** Instead of just Positive/Negative, classifying text on a 5-star scale (e.g., Very Negative, Negative, Neutral, Positive, Very Positive).
*   **Emotion Detection:** Moving beyond generic sentiment to detect specific human emotions (e.g., Anger, Joy, Sadness, Fear, Surprise).
*   **Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA):** The most complex and valuable form. It identifies *what* the user is talking about, and the sentiment applied specifically to that thing.
    *   *Review:* "The food was amazing but the waiter was terribly rude."
    *   *ABSA Output:* `[Food: POSITIVE]`, `[Service: NEGATIVE]`

<Callout icon="chart-line" title="The Challenge of Sarcasm">
Sarcasm remains one of the hardest unsolved problems in Sentiment Analysis. The sentence *"Oh great, my phone died again, best day ever!"* is heavily negative, but every single keyword ("great", "best day ever") is overwhelmingly positive. Only advanced LLMs with massive real-world context can reliably detect sarcasm.
</Callout>
