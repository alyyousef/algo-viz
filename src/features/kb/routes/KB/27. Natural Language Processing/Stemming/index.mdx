**Stemming** is a rudimentary text pre-processing technique used in traditional NLP. Its goal is to take a word and chop off its prefix or suffix to reduce it to its base "stem."

Before feeding text into an algorithm like Bag of Words, you want the computer to realize that "running," "runs," and "runner" are all essentially the same concept. Stemming achieves this by brutally chopping the ends off the words until they all match.

## How it Works: The Porter Stemmer

Stemming algorithms do not understand language, and they do not use dictionaries. They are simply massive lists of hard-coded regex rules (IF/THEN statements).

The most famous algorithm is the **Porter Stemmer** (invented by Martin Porter in 1980). It applies a sequential list of rules to the end of a word.

**Example Rules:**
*   Rule 1: If the word ends in "SSES", replace with "SS" (e.g., `caresses` $\rightarrow$ `caress`)
*   Rule 2: If the word ends in "IES", replace with "I" (e.g., `ponies` $\rightarrow$ `poni`)
*   Rule 3: If the word ends in "ING", delete it (e.g., `motoring` $\rightarrow$ `motor`)

## The Flaws of Stemming

Because stemming is blind and algorithmic, it makes massive, comical errors constantly. 

### 1. Over-stemming
The algorithm chops off too much, merging words that have completely different meanings.
*   "Universe" $\rightarrow$ `univers`
*   "University" $\rightarrow$ `univers`
*(The computer now thinks these two unrelated concepts are the exact same word).*

### 2. Under-stemming
The algorithm fails to chop enough off, leaving words separate that should be merged.
*   "Alumnus" $\rightarrow$ `alumnus`
*   "Alumni" $\rightarrow$ `alumni`

### 3. Irregular Verbs
Stemming algorithms completely fail on irregular verbs because they just chop endings off; they don't know grammar.
*   "Go" $\rightarrow$ `go`
*   "Went" $\rightarrow$ `went`
*(It has no idea these are the same verb).*

<Callout icon="code" title="Stemming vs Lemmatization">
Because of these flaws, Stemming is almost always taught alongside its smarter counterpart: **Lemmatization**. Lemmatization actually uses a dictionary and grammatical rules to find the true root word (e.g., converting "went" to "go"). However, stemming is significantly faster to compute, which is why it was the primary choice for massive search engines (like early Google or Elasticsearch) in the 1990s and 2000s when compute power was highly limited.
</Callout>
