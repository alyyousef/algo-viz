**Sequence Models** are a specific category of machine learning architectures designed to process data where the **order** of the data points matters.

Standard neural networks (like a Multi-Layer Perceptron) assume that every input is completely independent. If you feed it a picture of a cat, and then a picture of a dog, the network doesn't care about the order. 

However, many types of data are strictly sequential:
1.  **Text (NLP):** Words only make sense in order. ("Dog bites man" is very different from "Man bites dog").
2.  **Time Series:** Stock market prices or weather data where tomorrow's value depends entirely on yesterday's value.
3.  **Audio/Video:** Frames of a video only make sense when played sequentially.

Sequence Models are designed specifically to have an internal "memory" that allows them to remember previous inputs in the sequence to understand the current input.

## Classic Architectures

Historically, the field of Sequence Modeling was dominated by one primary architecture:

### Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)
An RNN processes a sequence one item at a time. As it reads "Word 1", it generates a "Hidden State" (a memory). When it reads "Word 2", it mathematically combines "Word 2" with the memory of "Word 1". 
*   **The Flaw:** Standard RNNs suffered from the *Vanishing Gradient Problem*. By the time they reached Word 100, they completely forgot Word 1. 

### LSTMs and GRUs
To fix the short-term memory of RNNs, researchers invented **Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)** networks and **Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs)**. These models introduced complex mathematical "gates" that allowed the network to explicitly choose what information to remember for a long time, and what to instantly forget. LSTMs ruled the NLP world from 2010 to 2017.

## The Paradigm Shift: Attention and Transformers

In 2017, the entire paradigm of Sequence Modeling was turned upside down.

Researchers realized that processing a sequence one step at a time (like an LSTM) was incredibly slow because it could not be parallelized on modern GPUs. You couldn't process word 50 until word 49 was finished.

They invented the **Transformer** architecture. Transformers do *not* process data sequentially. They process the entire sequence (e.g., all 100 words) simultaneously in a massive parallel matrix multiplication. 

To ensure the network still understood the "order" of the sequence, they invented **Positional Encoding**—a technique that mathematically tags every word with a number representing its position in the sentence.

<Callout icon="arrow-right" title="The Current State of the Art">
Today, LSTMs and RNNs are almost entirely obsolete for Natural Language Processing, having been completely replaced by Transformers (like GPT and BERT). However, LSTMs and highly optimized GRUs are still occasionally used for processing low-level numerical time-series data (like IoT sensor data or basic financial forecasting) where massive compute power is unavailable.
</Callout>
