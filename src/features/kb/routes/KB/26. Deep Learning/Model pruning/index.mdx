**Model Pruning** is a powerful compression technique used to make neural networks smaller, faster, and more energy-efficient, primarily so they can be deployed on edge devices (like smartphones, IoT sensors, or web browsers).

The core philosophy of pruning is simple: **Most of the weights in a massive neural network are useless.** 

## Why are weights useless?

When training a neural network, we usually over-parameterize it. We give it 100 million weights because that massive mathematical "search space" makes it much easier for Gradient Descent to find a good solution. 

However, once training is finished, if you look at the final weights, many of them will be extremely close to $0.00$. 
If a weight is near zero, multiplying data by it has almost no effect on the final output. That weight is dead weight. It takes up RAM and requires the CPU to perform a useless multiplication calculation.

## How Pruning Works

The pruning workflow typically follows a three-step cycle:

1.  **Train:** Train a massive, over-parameterized neural network until it achieves high accuracy.
2.  **Prune:** Look at all the weights in the network. Identify the bottom 20% of weights that are closest to $0.00$. Surgically delete them (force them to be exactly 0 and remove the connections).
3.  **Fine-Tune:** Because you just ripped out 20% of the network's brain, its accuracy will drop slightly. You train the network for a few more epochs to let the remaining 80% of the weights adjust and compensate for the missing connections.

You repeat steps 2 and 3 iteratively. In many cases, you can prune **up to 90%** of a neural network's weights without losing a single percentage point of accuracy.

## Types of Pruning

### 1. Unstructured Pruning (Weight Pruning)
This is the method described above. You look at every single weight individually and delete the lowest ones, regardless of where they are.
**The Problem:** Unstructured pruning leaves the matrices looking like Swiss cheese (Sparse Matrices). Standard CPUs and GPUs are heavily optimized for dense blocks of math. Skipping random zeros in a matrix is often *slower* than just multiplying by zero. Therefore, unstructured pruning makes the file size smaller (good for downloading), but rarely speeds up inference times.

### 2. Structured Pruning (Neuron/Filter Pruning)
Instead of deleting individual weights, Structured Pruning deletes entire neurons, entire attention heads, or entire convolutional filters at once.
If an entire row or column in a matrix is removed, the remaining matrix shrinks into a smaller, dense, perfect rectangle. 
**The Benefit:** Standard hardware can process this smaller rectangle natively, resulting in massive, real-world speedups and memory savings.

<Callout icon="scissors" title="The Lottery Ticket Hypothesis">
In 2019, a famous paper proposed the *Lottery Ticket Hypothesis*. It proved that inside every massive, randomly initialized neural network, there exists a tiny, sparse sub-network (a "winning ticket") that could have achieved the exact same accuracy if trained in isolation. The only reason we train massive networks is that buying a billion tickets is the only way to guarantee we find the winning one. Pruning is the act of throwing away the losing tickets after the draw.
</Callout>
