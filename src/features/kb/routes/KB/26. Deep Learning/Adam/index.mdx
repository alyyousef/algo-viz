**Adam** (Adaptive Moment Estimation) is an optimization algorithm used to update the weights of a neural network iteratively based on training data. Since its introduction in 2014 by Diederik Kingma and Jimmy Ba, it has become the default optimizer for almost all deep learning tasks.

Adam works by combining the best ideas from two earlier optimization algorithms: **Momentum** and **RMSProp**.

## The Two Ingredients of Adam

To understand Adam, you must understand the two concepts it merges:

### 1. Momentum (The "Heavy Ball")
Standard Gradient Descent simply calculates the slope and takes a step down the hill. If the hill is bumpy, it gets stuck.
**Momentum** remembers the *past* gradients (the past direction). Imagine rolling a heavy bowling ball down a hill; even if it hits a small flat spot or a tiny bump, its momentum carries it forward. 

Mathematically, this is the **First Moment** (the mean of the gradients). It speeds up training when moving in a consistent direction and smooths out erratic, bouncy gradients.

### 2. RMSProp (Adaptive Learning Rates)
Standard Gradient Descent applies the exact same learning rate to every single weight in the network. If one feature is incredibly rare (like the word "xylophone" in a text dataset), its weight will almost never get updated.
**RMSProp** fixes this by giving *every single weight its own unique learning rate*. It keeps a running average of the *squared* gradients. If a weight gets updated frequently, RMSProp aggressively shrinks its learning rate. If a weight is rarely updated, RMSProp boosts its learning rate so that when it finally sees data, it takes a massive step.

Mathematically, this is the **Second Moment** (the uncentered variance of the gradients).

## How Adam Merges Them

Adam literally computes both:
1.  It calculates the exponential moving average of the gradients (Momentum).
2.  It calculates the exponential moving average of the squared gradients (RMSProp).

Before applying them, Adam applies a **Bias Correction**. Because both moving averages are initialized at 0, they are heavily biased toward 0 during the very first few steps of training. Bias correction mathematically forces them to act like true averages immediately, preventing the model from getting stuck at the very beginning of training.

Finally, Adam updates the weights by dividing the Momentum term by the square root of the RMSProp term.

<Callout icon="gear" title="Default Hyperparameters">
Adam is famous for working incredibly well "out of the box" with its default hyperparameters, which rarely need tuning:
*   **Learning Rate (alpha):** $0.001$ (This is the one you *should* tune).
*   **Beta 1 (Momentum decay):** $0.9$
*   **Beta 2 (RMSProp decay):** $0.999$
*   **Epsilon:** $10^{-8}$ (A tiny number added to the denominator to prevent dividing by zero).
</Callout>

## Summary

Adam is the gold standard of optimizers. By combining the speed-boosting properties of Momentum with the adaptive, per-weight learning rates of RMSProp, it converges on the optimal weights drastically faster and more reliably than standard Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD).
