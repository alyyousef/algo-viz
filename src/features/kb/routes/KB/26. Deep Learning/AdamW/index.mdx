**AdamW** is a widely used optimization algorithm that modifies the standard Adam optimizer. Introduced in 2017 by Ilya Loshchilov and Frank Hutter, AdamW fixes a major mathematical flaw in how standard Adam handles **L2 Regularization (Weight Decay)**.

Today, AdamW is the standard optimizer used to train almost all cutting-edge Transformer models, including Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision Transformers.

## The Flaw in Standard Adam

To prevent neural networks from overfitting, we often use **Weight Decay** (L2 Regularization). The goal is simple: gently push all the weights in the network toward zero. 

In standard Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), you apply weight decay by modifying the Loss Function. You add a penalty based on the squared sum of the weights. When you calculate the gradient of this new loss function, the weight decay mathematically acts as a simple subtraction from the weights. 

**However, the original Adam optimizer calculates moving averages of the gradients.**

If you apply L2 regularization inside the standard Adam loss function, Adam takes that weight decay penalty and mixes it into its complex moving averages (the Momentum and RMSProp terms). 
Because RMSProp aggressively shrinks the learning rate for weights with large gradients, **it ends up shrinking the weight decay penalty for the exact weights that need it the most.** 

The result? Standard Adam fails to regularize the model properly, leading to worse generalization (overfitting) compared to standard SGD.

## The AdamW Fix (Decoupled Weight Decay)

AdamW fixes this with a brilliant but simple idea: **Decouple the weight decay from the gradient calculations.**

Instead of adding the L2 penalty to the loss function, AdamW:
1.  Calculates the standard gradients based *only* on the error of the model.
2.  Passes those clean gradients through all the complex Adam math (Momentum and RMSProp) to calculate the primary weight update.
3.  **Then, at the very end, simply subtracts a tiny fraction of the weight directly.**

By moving the weight decay step *outside* of Adam's adaptive learning rate calculations, AdamW ensures that the regularization penalty is applied equally and effectively to all weights, exactly as it is in standard SGD.

<Callout icon="chart-line" title="Why AdamW Matters">
For years, researchers believed that while Adam trained models *faster*, standard SGD trained models that *generalized better* to unseen data. The paper introducing AdamW proved that this was solely due to Adam's broken implementation of weight decay. With AdamW, you get the blazing speed of Adam *and* the superior generalization of SGD.
</Callout>

## Summary

If you are training a modern, complex neural network (especially a Transformer) and you intend to use weight decay to prevent overfitting, you should never use standard `Adam`. You should always use `AdamW`.
