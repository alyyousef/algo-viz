*(Note: This module focuses on the architecture of video generation. For the conceptual impact of the technology, see the **Text-to-Video** module).*

For years, AI video generation was locked behind severe limitations. Models could only generate a few seconds of blurry, glitchy footage before breaking down. In February 2024, OpenAI released **Sora**, a model that fundamentally shifted the paradigm by generating 60-second, photorealistic videos that perfectly obeyed the laws of physics.

Sora achieved this by combining two massive architectures that had previously been kept separate: **Diffusion** (used in Midjourney) and **Transformers** (used in ChatGPT).

## The Architecture: Diffusion Transformers (DiT)

To understand how Sora works, you have to understand how it views video.

### 1. Spacetime Patches (The Video Tokens)
When you train a Large Language Model, you break a book down into "Tokens" (chunks of text).
When OpenAI trained Sora, they broke millions of YouTube videos down into **Spacetime Patches**. 

A Spacetime Patch is a 3D block of data. It contains a small chunk of space (a 16x16 square of pixels) stretching across a small chunk of time (a few frames). Sora treats these 3D chunks of video exactly the same way ChatGPT treats text words.

### 2. The Transformer Engine
Because Sora treats video chunks like words, it uses a massive Transformer architecture. This allows the model to leverage the same **Attention Mechanism** that makes LLMs so smart.
If Sora generates a red car in Patch 1, the Attention mechanism ensures that Patch 500 (10 seconds later in the video) mathematically remembers the red car and draws it perfectly, solving the massive problem of Object Permanence that plagued older video models.

### 3. The Diffusion Generator
While the Transformer handles the "logic" and the timeline of the video, Sora still uses Diffusion (un-NOISING) to actually paint the pixels. 
Sora starts with a 60-second block of pure, random television static. The Transformer looks at the text prompt, coordinates the timeline, and guides the Diffusion process to slowly remove the static until a photorealistic video emerges.

<Callout icon="globe" title="World Simulators">
OpenAI does not classify Sora as a video generator; they classify it as a **"World Simulator."** Because Sora was trained on millions of hours of real-world video, the Transformer mathematically reverse-engineered the laws of physics. If you prompt Sora to draw a man taking a bite of a burger, Sora automatically calculates the physics of the bite, generating the correct bite mark on the bun without ever being explicitly programmed to understand what a "bite" is.
</Callout>
