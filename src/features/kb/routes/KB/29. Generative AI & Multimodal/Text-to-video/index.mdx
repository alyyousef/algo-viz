*(Note: This module focuses on the overarching concept of generating video from text. For details on the architecture behind models like Sora, see the **Video Generation** module).*

**Text-to-Video (T2V)** is the process of generating a completely synthetic video clip from a purely textual description (e.g., *"A golden retriever driving a red sports car through a cyberpunk city"*).

While Text-to-Image was solved in 2022, Text-to-Video remained incredibly primitive until 2024. Early models generated blurry, warped messes that looked like fever dreams. The challenge was not generating the pixels—it was generating *physics*.

## The Physics Problem

An image generator only has to draw a car once. 
A video generator drawing a 10-second clip at 30 frames per second has to draw that same car **300 times**.

More importantly, it has to draw it moving through space. It has to understand:
1.  **Object Permanence:** If the car drives behind a building, the car doesn't cease to exist. It must emerge on the other side of the building looking exactly the same. Early models failed this completely; objects would constantly morph into other objects.
2.  **3D Geometry:** As the camera pans around the car, the neural network has to mathematically understand how light and shadows change across a 3D surface, even though the AI has never existed in the physical 3D world.
3.  **Real-world Physics:** If the car hits a puddle, water must splash. The AI has to "hallucinate" the physics of fluid dynamics.

## Current T2V Capabilities

In early 2024, models like OpenAI's **Sora** and Kling AI shattered expectations.

*   **Shot Length:** Early models could only generate 3 seconds of video before collapsing into a glitchy mess. Modern models can generate up to 60 seconds of flawless, temporally consistent video.
*   **Camera Control:** Users can use prompt engineering to act as cinematographers, explicitly commanding the AI to perform complex drone shots, macro-closeups, or sweeping pans (*"A low-angle tracking shot..."*).
*   **Hyper-Realism:** The models have become so advanced at simulating light bouncing off water and skin that the resulting videos are completely indistinguishable from real, high-budget Hollywood drone footage.

<Callout icon="clapperboard" title="The Death of B-Roll">
The immediate, real-world impact of Text-to-Video is the disruption of the stock footage industry (B-Roll). Instead of a video editor paying \$200 to license a stock video of "a man drinking coffee on a rainy day," they can now generate the exact clip they need, with the exact lighting they want, for pennies using AI.
</Callout>
