**Natural Language Processing (NLP)** is the branch of AI focused on giving computers the ability to understand text and spoken words in much the same way human beings can.
It bridges the gap between human communication and computer understanding.

## 1. The Historical Evolution of NLP

The field of NLP has undergone three massive paradigm shifts.

### Era 1: Rule-Based / Symbolic NLP (1950s - 1980s)
Early systems relied on linguists hardcoding grammar rules and vocabulary into expert systems (e.g., regex patterns).
- **Pros:** Highly interpretable.
- **Cons:** Human language is far too ambiguous, contextual, and fluid to be captured by rigid IF-THEN rules.

### Era 2: Statistical NLP (1990s - 2010s)
Instead of hardcoding rules, researchers used Machine Learning (like Naive Bayes or Hidden Markov Models) on large text corpora to count the frequencies of words and calculate probabilities. 
- **Techniques:** TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency), Bag of Words, N-grams.
- **Pros:** Scalable and robust to typos/slang.
- **Cons:** Completely ignored the *semantics* (meaning) and word order. "The dog bit the man" and "The man bit the dog" often looked identical to a Bag of Words model.

### Era 3: Deep Learning & Neural NLP (2010s - Present)
The current era, dominated by Neural Networks, Embeddings, and Transformers.

## 2. Core Concepts in Neural NLP

To use Machine Learning on text, you must first convert words into numbers.

### Tokenization
The process of breaking a raw string of text into smaller chunks called **Tokens**. Tokens can be words, sub-words, or characters.
- Sentence: `"ChatGPT is amazing!"`
- Sub-word tokens (e.g., Byte-Pair Encoding): `["Chat", "G", "PT", " is", " amazing", "!"]`

### Word Embeddings
Instead of treating each word as an isolated ID, Neural NLP maps words to dense vectors (e.g., arrays of 300 floating-point numbers) in a high-dimensional space.
Words with similar meanings are mathematically closer together in this space. 
- Famous property of embeddings (Word2Vec): `Vector("King") - Vector("Man") + Vector("Woman") ≈ Vector("Queen")`.

### Contextual Embeddings (Transformers)
Older embeddings (like Word2Vec) assigned a single static vector to the word "bank", regardless of whether it meant "river bank" or "financial bank".
Modern NLP uses the **Attention Mechanism** (via the Transformer architecture) to read the entire sentence and dynamically adjust the embedding of "bank" based on the surrounding words.

## 3. Common NLP Tasks

- **Sentiment Analysis:** Classifying text as positive, negative, or neutral. (e.g., analyzing movie reviews).
- **Named Entity Recognition (NER):** Identifying and extracting entities like People, Organizations, and Locations from text.
- **Machine Translation:** Translating text from one language to another (e.g., Google Translate).
- **Text Summarization:** Condensing a long article into a short summary.
- **Question Answering:** Given a context document and a question, extracting or generating the correct answer.

<Callout icon="info" title="Large Language Models (LLMs)">
LLMs (like GPT-4 or Claude) are technically performing the ultimate NLP task: **Next-Token Prediction**. By simply trying to predict the most statistically likely next word over and over across trillions of words of internet text, these models develop a surprisingly deep understanding of grammar, facts, reasoning, and coding.
</Callout>
