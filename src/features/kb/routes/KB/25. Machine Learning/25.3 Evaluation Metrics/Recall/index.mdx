**Recall** (also known as Sensitivity, or True Positive Rate) is a fundamental metric for evaluating classification models. It answers a very specific question:

*"Out of all the actual Positive instances in the real world, how many did our model successfully find?"*

In simpler terms, it measures the model's ability to **catch everything** without missing any targets.

## The Formula

Recall is calculated using values from the Confusion Matrix:

$$\text{Recall} = \frac{\text{True Positives (TP)}}{\text{True Positives (TP)} + \text{False Negatives (FN)}}$$

*   **True Positives:** The person had cancer, and the model predicted cancer.
*   **False Negatives (Misses):** The person had cancer, but the model missed it and predicted they were healthy.

If a model makes zero False Negatives, its recall is a perfect 1.0 (100%).

## When to Optimize for Recall

You should optimize your model for Recall when the **cost of a False Negative (a miss) is extremely high**, even if it means generating a lot of False Positives (false alarms).

<Callout icon="suitcase-medical" title="Example: Medical Diagnosis">
Imagine building an AI to scan X-rays for early signs of cancer.

*   A **False Positive (False Alarm):** The model flags a healthy patient as having cancer. This causes stress and requires a follow-up biopsy, which is inconvenient and expensive, but the patient is ultimately fine.
*   A **False Negative (A Miss):** The model tells a patient with early-stage cancer that they are perfectly healthy. The cancer goes untreated and the patient dies.

Because a False Negative is fatal, you want a model with incredibly high Recall. You want the model to flag *anything* that looks even remotely suspicious, prioritizing catching every true case over worrying about false alarms.
</Callout>

Other examples where Recall is paramount:
*   **Fraud Detection:** It is better to temporarily freeze a legitimate credit card transaction (False Positive) than to let a hacker drain a bank account (False Negative).
*   **Factory Quality Control:** It is better to throw away a perfectly good microchip (False Positive) than to put a defective chip into an airplane (False Negative).

## The Tradeoff with Precision

Recall is inherently tied to **Precision** (Positive Predictive Value).

*   **Recall:** Quantity of actual positives found. (Minimizes False Negatives).
*   **Precision:** Quality of positive predictions. (Minimizes False Positives).

You can easily build a model with 100% Recall: just have it predict "Positive" for every single instance. It will catch 100% of the targets, but its Precision will be near 0% because it generated millions of False Positives. 

Because you usually cannot maximize both simultaneously, they are often combined into the **F1 Score** or evaluated across all thresholds using a **Precision-Recall Curve**.
