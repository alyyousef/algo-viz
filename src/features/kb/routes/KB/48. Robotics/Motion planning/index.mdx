<ConceptTemplate title="Motion Planning & Trajectory Generation">

**Path Planning** mathematically draws a line on a map from Point A to Point B. But a line is just geometry. 

**Motion Planning** takes that line and adds Time, Velocity, and Acceleration constraints. It answers the question: *"Exactly how fast should the wheels spin at millisecond 400 to follow this line without the robot physically tipping over?"*

<Callout icon="tip" title="The Jerk Constraint">
  In physics, the derivative of Acceleration is called **Jerk**. 
  
  If a robot accelerates instantly from 0 to 10 mph, the Jerk is mathematically infinite. The robot's physical gears will violently grind and snap. Motion Planning algorithms mathematically generate smooth "S-Curve" velocity profiles to ensure that Acceleration ramps up slowly, keeping Jerk physically manageable.
</Callout>

## Obstacle Avoidance (Local Planning)

The global path plan says "Go down the hallway." But suddenly, a human steps into the hallway. 

The Local Motion Planner (like the **Dynamic Window Approach - DWA**) constantly runs at 20 Hz. It mathematically simulates 50 different arc-shaped trajectories the robot could drive in the next 1 second. It scores each trajectory (Penalizing driving near the human, rewarding driving toward the hallway goal), picks the best mathematical score, and executes it.
