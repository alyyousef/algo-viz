A robot must mathematically understand two completely different physical realities:
1. Where is the world?
2. Where are my own legs?


<Callout icon="tip" title="Proprioception (Self-Awareness)">


  Humans mathematically know where their arms are, even with their eyes closed. Robots achieve this using **Proprioceptive Sensors**.
  - **Encoders:** Optical discs attached to the motors that mathematically count exact degrees of rotation, allowing the robot to know its exact joint angles.
  - **IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit):** Accelerometers and Gyroscopes that mathematically tell the robot if it is currently falling over, allowing it to balance.


</Callout>


## Exteroception (World-Awareness)

To navigate the world without hitting a wall, robots use **Exteroceptive Sensors**.
- **LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging):** A spinning laser that shoots millions of light pulses per second, mathematically measuring the exact time-of-flight of the photons bouncing back to generate a perfect 3D point-cloud of the room.
- **Ultrasonic / Sonar:** Shooting sound waves and measuring the echo. Cheap, but mathematically fuzzy.
- **Stereo Cameras:** Using two cameras (like human eyes) to mathematically calculate depth perception based on pixel offset.
