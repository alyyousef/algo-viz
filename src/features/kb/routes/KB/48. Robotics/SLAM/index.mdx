<ConceptTemplate title="SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping)">

If you drop a Roomba vacuum in a brand new house, it faces a massive mathematical paradox:
1. To know where it is, it needs a Map.
2. To build a Map, it needs to know exactly where it is.

Solving this paradox is called **SLAM**. It is arguably the most famous and mathematically difficult problem in modern robotics.

<Callout icon="error" title="The Odometry Drift Problem">
  Why can't the robot just count its wheel rotations (Odometry) to know where it is? 
  Because wheels slip. If the robot's wheels slip on a rug by just 1 millimeter, that mathematical error accumulates. After 10 minutes of driving, the robot mathematically believes it is in the kitchen, but it is physically crashing into the living room wall.
</Callout>

## The SLAM Solution

SLAM algorithms (like Gmapping or Cartographer) solve this using probabilistic mathematics (Particle Filters or Graph Optimization). 

As the robot drives, its LiDAR scans the walls. It mathematically aligns the *current* LiDAR scan with the *previous* LiDAR scan to correct the wheel slip. Slowly, it builds a massive 2D or 3D grid mathematically proving both the shape of the room and the robot's exact XY coordinate within that room.
